# Daylight extra-time recommendations: output template and rules

Extra recommendations suggest useful work when the user wants more beyond the main list. They may include excluded work and other work worth starting early. Criteria are intentionally flexible, without the main list's strict decision structure.

## Output template

For any additional time, I recommend [task or commitment work], [task or commitment work], and [task or commitment work].

[First recommendation] would [specific benefit of early progress], because [relevant upcoming commitments, future demands, progress report or notes].

[Second recommendation] is worth getting a head start on because [uncertainty about remaining work/difficulty or another supported benefit].

[Explain other recommendations only as needed to make the benefit clear.]
Use natural wording, not mechanical repetition. Placeholders do not require three items; include only as many as have useful explanations.
Example: For any additional time, I recommend starting the presentation and reviewing the remaining PCB #2 requirements. Getting ahead on the presentation would leave less to think about tomorrow, when your work shift and band practice limit availability. Your PCB #2 notes have no estimate, and PCB #1 was harder than expected according to your latest report. Looking at PCB #2 early could reveal similar difficulties while room remains to respond. This is possible difficulty, not a claim PCB #2 will necessarily take longer.

## 1. Explicit request and separate bank

Only generate after the separate small extra-time button is pressed. Main-list generation alone does not trigger extras. Do not append extras automatically. Results appear in a bank clearly marked separate from the main list. They enter the main list only when the user explicitly adds them.

## 2. Candidates and flexible criteria

Candidates include excluded work, other recorded tasks, and recorded deadline/project work benefiting from an early start. Excluded work is not the complete boundary. Reasons include non-urgent important work; useful early starts; reduced tomorrow demands; freeing mental space around upcoming commitments; uncertain remaining effort; possible difficulty beyond current notes; useful future flexibility. Examples are not a strict checklist. A general head start can suffice without a strong main-list inclusion reason.

## 3. Explain benefit

Explain what the user gains: less remaining work tomorrow, fewer competing demands around a fixed commitment, less unfinished work to keep in mind, earlier discovery of difficulty, earlier understanding of remaining effort, more room before a deadline if difficult, or a head start on important work. Use concrete records when available. Example: Advancing presentation reduces tomorrow's remaining work when your appointment and evening shift reduce work opportunities. If only a general head start is supported, say so; never manufacture urgency. Reduced headspace demands are a possible benefit, not knowledge of the user's feelings.

## 4. Reports across days

Use latest relevant report for EACH task/aspect, not just yesterday. Example: Monday outline complete/slides unstarted; Tuesday essay-only report; Wednesday may use Monday's presentation evidence. Tuesday does not erase it. Use accomplishments to identify useful remaining work; session completion alone is not completion. Reports stay open-ended. If postponement information appears in relevant reports use it; if absent, do not invent it.

## 5. Uncertain effort/difficulty

Uncertainty can justify starting early. Distinguish explicitly reported uncertainty, absent estimates and related work reported harder than expected from AI interpretation of those facts. Do not turn possibility into fact.
Appropriate: No remaining-work estimate, so an early start may reveal size before the deadline is close.
Appropriate: First board needed more rework, so early second-board work could reveal similar problems.
Unsupported: Second board will take longer than you think.
Never invent effort estimates or measured pace.

## 6. Future commitments

Future blocks can justify optional early work even when excluded reasonably from main list. Explain how progress leaves less for tomorrow or another upcoming day when named commitments limit availability. A commitment does not justify every recommendation; connect it to this work. Flag missing/stale calendar data where material.

## 7. Excluded work remains optional

Exclusion does not mean no benefit. Good biology progress may leave sufficient buffer; essay has stronger attention needs; biology is omitted. After explicit extra request, biology can be an optional head start. Explain it can wait but early progress has a particular benefit. Do not suddenly call it urgent without new evidence.
Example: Biology can still wait based on your last report. With additional time, remaining revision leaves less for tomorrow alongside scheduled commitments.

## 8. No durations

No short/medium/long suggestions, suggested minutes or prescribed sessions. Explain usefulness; user chooses duration. Recorded estimates and calendar times remain evidence.

## 9. Recorded work

Recommend actual tasks, commitments or established aspects, never invented components. Distinct recorded PCB #2 may be considered alongside main PCB #1. Avoid presenting the exact same work as a new extra. For further work under the same commitment, identify the additional recorded work.

## 10. Optional recommendations

Answer the explicit request for worthwhile extra work. Serious delay risk is unnecessary; a useful head start suffices. Never schedule or alter commitments. Proposed dates are unapproved drafts. If records support no useful extra recommendation, say so briefly rather than fill arbitrarily.

## Example cases

- Excluded after good progress: optional early work, preserve can-wait rationale.
- Tomorrow shift/appointment: recommend relevant work reducing demands, name constraints.
- Important work without estimate: early start may reveal size.
- Related work harder than expected: may reveal similar problems, not certainty.
- Next recorded aspect unstarted/nonurgent: useful head start without invented urgency.
- Latest candidate report several days old: use it, not a newer unrelated report.
- Main request only: no extras.
- Separate button pressed: generate optional bank using flexible criteria.
